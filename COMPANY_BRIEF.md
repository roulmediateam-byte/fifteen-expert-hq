# Company brief: The $15-an-Hour Expert

## The idea in plain words
When a doctor's office sends a bill to an insurance company, the insurer often says no, and the office loses money it is owed. Most offices never fight back, because an appeal takes time and an expert who knows the rules. We let AI do the expert part: it reads the rejection, works out why it happened, and writes the letter asking the insurer to pay. An ordinary trained worker checks it and sends it. This matches YC's Winter 2027 request "The $15-an-Hour Expert" (Brad Flora): AI that turns an ordinary worker into an expert (https://www.ycombinator.com/rfs).

## How it earns
We only get paid when we win. The insurer pays the practice; then we invoice the practice either a share of what was recovered or a flat fee per win (a US lawyer must confirm which is allowed; Medicare bars billing agents from charging a share of collections, 42 CFR 424.73). Example (our estimate): a US$200 claim at a 20% fee earns us US$40.

## How big (US)
- Doctor and clinic spending: US$1.1 trillion in 2024 (Medical Economics, CMS data).
- 47% of doctors work in practices of 10 or fewer (AMA 2024).
- 12% to 15% of claims are denied at first (Kodiak 2024; Premier).
- Our estimates: about US$10 billion a year could be won back for small practices; a 20% fee on all of it is about US$2 billion a year; a realistic big outcome is US$20 to US$100 million a year (1% to 5% share); the three founders alone, at about 1,000 appeals a month, about US$240,000 a year.

## Competitors
